# Dictionary review: proposals

Stage 1 of [DICTIONARY-REVIEW.md](../DICTIONARY-REVIEW.md), 12 October 2026. **Nothing has been changed yet.** Reply with anything to leave out, change or add, or "approved".

## Summary

All 31 categories were reviewed, then given a second pass for completeness: the owner asked for every answer a player is likely to think of, with no limit per category. Every proposed word's status was checked against the real data. In total:

- **About 1,440 removes**, most of them from the *also* lists (everyday words that only fit through an obscure WordNet sense, such as *permit* as a fish or *world* as a horse). Counts include plural forms.
- **About 5,630 adds**: about 1,480 promoted from *also*, 1,590 already in the dictionary but in neither list, and 2,560 NOT IN DICTIONARY. Those would go into `extra-words.txt` with their plurals, so the dictionary would grow by roughly 5,000 words, from about 32,500. They become playable in every prompt, not just their category.
- **Three label changes**: kitchen, weather and landform.
- **15 new categories**, measured against WordNet.

### The biggest problems found

1. **The first answers people give are missing from *main*, nearly everywhere.** WordNet files the word's main meaning somewhere else, so it lands in *also* or nowhere. Examples: cod, haddock and tuna (fish); banana and cherry (fruit); carrot and onion (vegetable); oak, ash and birch (tree); hammer and saw (tool); bus and van (vehicle); table (furniture); chess and cricket (sport); worry and guilt (feeling); fog and frost (weather).
2. **Some obvious answers aren't accepted at all.** *Train* isn't a vehicle. *Murder* isn't a crime. *Farmer*, *policeman*, *builder*, *engineer*, *actor* and *priest* aren't jobs. *Water* and *juice* aren't drinks. *Bomb* and *grenade* aren't weapons. *Fridge* and *sink* aren't in kitchen, and *cupboard* and *shelf* aren't furniture. *Office*, *shop* and *factory* aren't buildings. Music has no named dance at all, and no rock, pop or jazz in *main*.
3. **Many everyday words aren't in the dictionary**, so they can't be played anywhere. Examples: plaice, hake, kingfisher, chaffinch, courgette, aubergine, nectarine, prosecco, hob, colander, glockenspiel, maracas, foxglove, snowdrop, optician, anorak and covid. Many plurals are missing too (kiwis, gins, lagers, curries), so a plural answer is rejected even when the singular is fine.
4. **Some categories are swallowed by another.** Every drink is also in food, so *vodka* counts as a food. Animal is built on its own, so fixes to mammal, bird, fish and insect don't reach it (*britt* and *lotte*, removed from fish, are still animals).

### Things to decide besides the word lists

- **Decided: categories become hand-kept files.** They will no longer be generated from WordNet. Each category becomes a plain file in the repository (`data/categories/<id>.txt`), and the approved changes go straight into those files. The overrides file goes away. WordNet is only used to draft a new category, once. See stage 2 in [DICTIONARY-REVIEW.md](../DICTIONARY-REVIEW.md).
- **Decided: drinks are not food.** Food and drink stay separate categories. With hand-kept files, the drinks are simply deleted from food (keeping milk and chocolate, which are both).
- **Decided: animal includes the other four automatically.** `animal.txt` keeps only the animals that fit none of mammal, bird, fish or insect (snakes, frogs, crabs, worms, young animals and so on). The build adds the words of the other four, so each fix is made once.
- **Plurals left over from earlier fixes:** a `-word` override doesn't remove the plural, so some leftovers are still accepted: *professionals*, *academics*, *coles*, *quiets*, *wormholes*, *cavities*, *pests*. The proposals list them, and they go when the files are cleaned up.
- **Two earlier overrides do nothing:** `+cyan` and `+ochre` (colour), because neither word is in the dictionary. They need adding to `extra-words.txt`.
- **Decided: blocking only goes downwards.** A blocked word blocks the forms made from it (*cocks*, *cocked*), never the word it appears to come from. That frees *pub* and *pubs* (wrongly read as the root of *pubes*), *hump*, *humps*, *humped* and *sod*, *sods*.
- **Decided: slurs, strong swearing and sexual crimes stay blocked** (nigger, kike, faggot, retard; fuck, cunt, shit, motherfucker; rape, incest, pedophile and so on).
- **Open: mild rudeness, body and sex terms, sex work** (arse, bitch, dick, cock, prick, tit, pubes, nipple, penis, orgasm, porn, whore, hooker, bimbo, genital and so on). The owner doesn't find these very offensive but doesn't want them shown on the front page. **Decided: a "masked" list**, built after this review: words on it are accepted and score normally, show in the feed with only the first and last letters (*p\*\*\*s*), and are never used in prompts or allowed in nicknames. These words move to it then (the exact list to be shown to the owner first). Until then they stay blocked.
- **Decided: no limit on adds.** Every answer a player is likely to think of goes in, because missing ones are frustrating and turn into suggestions to approve. The lists were extended in a second pass.
- **Decided: ordinary words only, not names.** A word is allowed if it's an ordinary word that a dictionary lists in lower case. Taken out of the adds: cluedo, jenga, othello, yahtzee, pictionary, kerplunk, rummikub, uno, subbuteo, poohsticks, macarena, zumba, motown, lightsaber, popsicle, bakewell, mellotron, gatling, jetski, jetskiing. Kept, as brands that became ordinary words: frisbee, taser, stetson, babygro, pianola, clingfilm, pilates, kalashnikov, uzi, semtex. Kept, as words named after places: wines (chianti, rioja), cheeses (stilton, gouda), breeds (alsatian, persian), the charleston.
- **Decided: no abbreviations** (hiv, mrsa, copd, ibs, ocd, adhd, ptsd, cjd, bse, sars, tnt, bmx taken out). *Covid* stays, as an ordinary word now. Alzheimers, parkinsons, huntingtons and crohns stay in main: they're what people say, typed without the apostrophe.
- **Decided: phrases run together go in *also*.** Where the usual form is two words or hyphenated (*tea towel*, *forget-me-not*, *T-shirt*), the joined-up form (*teatowel*) is accepted but not counted. Where it's normally one word (*eggcup*, *wineglass*), it counts as usual. Stage 2 sorts every such word in the adds this way.
- *Horniness* is held back for the masked list, as *horny* is blocked.
- **Hyphenated words** (in-laws, half-sister) can't be played at all. That is how the game works, not a list problem. A later idea, noted in docs/DESIGN.md: join up what a player types, so "tea towel" or "T-shirt" is read as *teatowel* or *tshirt*.

## New categories

Sizes were measured, not estimated. NLTK and WordNet were installed and a throwaway script loaded `scripts/build-categories.py` itself (its `main_senses` and `all_senses`), ran it over every word in `data/words.txt` for each candidate's synsets, and left out blocklisted words. The counts below are distinct singular answers (a plural whose singular is also listed is counted once); `category-report.php` counts plural forms too, so its figures would be about a third higher (given in brackets). "Main" is before any overrides, so it still includes the odd members named under Caveats, and the everyday answers that are only in also need `+` overrides to count. Example answers marked (also) are in the dictionary but only in also; (not in dictionary) means extra-words.txt is needed.
