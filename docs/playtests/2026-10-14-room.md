# Playtest: a room, 14 October 2026

The first playtest of a room, the first on the new rarity curve (`1 + 2 × p²`), and a check of the Kitchen Cup fixes. Eve pressed "Join a room" on the main page in Chrome at 23:37 and opened **Room 1** (`4fa81784`, the site's first room). Ada, Ben, Cal and Dee joined through the API (a new `room` command in the kit, the same call as the button) and all landed in Room 1; the owner joined too, as Mimamo. The agents played at a human pace from 23:38 to about 00:05, across the big world's midnight change. Room prompts kept their normal length (up to 24 hours, ending early at 80% said), as the owner chose.

Standings at 00:05 (the room carries on): **Dee 217, Ben 158, Ada 141, Eve 123, Cal 109, Mimamo 32.** Three prompts: "Starts and ends with B" (15 answers, about 2 minutes), "A boat or ship of exactly 5 letters" (10 answers, about a minute), "An insect or bug of 8+ letters" (77 answers, still running with 27 unsaid).

## Prompts too small for a room

Rooms pick prompts with 10 to 80 answers (`Prompts::pick`'s defaults). With six players, the 15-answer prompt went in about 2 minutes and the 10-answer one in about a minute: most players managed a word or two, and Ben "could not realistically win a 10 to 15 word prompt at human pace". The 77-answer insect prompt was the fun one for everyone ("a proper word-lover's game", Dee). In a room of 60 a 10-answer prompt would go in seconds, and the 24-hour clock means little either way.

**Suggestion:** rooms ask for bigger prompts, say 40 to 200 answers (as the big world asks for 60 to 400), so a prompt lasts a while and a slower player gets a turn.

## Irregular plurals aren't one family

`WordFamilies` groups words by suffix rules only (plurals with -s/-es, -ed, -ing), so **no irregular plural is linked to its singular**: knife/knives, wolf/wolves, leaf/leaves, mouse/mice, louse/lice, woodlouse/woodlice, dormouse/dormice, goose/geese, tooth/teeth, foot/feet, child/children, man/men, woman/women, fireman/firemen, person/people, cactus/cacti, fungus/fungi (checked in `data/dictionary.php`: *mouse*'s family is *mouse, mouses*). So:

- on a letter prompt, *knife* and *knives* both score in full, and saying one leaves the other free;
- on a list prompt, the irregular form is refused: Eve's *woodlice* for "An insect or bug" got "isn't on our list" (the list takes regular plurals only).

**Fix:** a list of irregular plurals (and other irregular forms, such as *ran* and *went*) linked into families at build time, so each pair is one answer, with the singular's rarity.

## Rarity: British and closed-compound words still score high

On the new curve, the words that still look too rare are familiar British ones, and most are **closed compounds** (written as one word, which subtitles often split): *housefly* ×2.2 (scored 20), *horsefly* ×2.2 (17), *greenfly* ×2.4 (17), *whitefly* ×2.6 (21), *hoverfly* ×2.7 (22), *silverfish* ×2.1 (19), *woodworm* ×2.1 (18), *woodlouse* ×2.4 (18), *bluebottle* ×2.3 (19), *tortoiseshell* ×2.3 (41), *mealworm* ×2.6 (23). Compare *ladybird* and *dragonfly* at 9. The prompt bonus adds to it (up to ×1.65 late in a prompt), so a familiar word said late can score like a rare one said early; that's by design, but it makes these stand out.

This backs the rarity review's open item: fixing British words with a British frequency list rather than by hand. Placing a closed compound by its parts' counts (or the count of the spaced form, "house fly") would catch the rest.

Two-word answers score low by design (a phrase takes its longest word's rarity): *stag beetle* 6, *stick insect* 6, *daddy-long-legs* 6, *crane fly* 6. Ben and Dee found that sensible.

## Penalties: consistent, but they feel harsh

Ben and Cal reported penalties as inconsistent. Checked against `Game::play`, they follow the rules:

- **Someone else's word costs 2, unless it was said in the last minute** (or it's yours, a team-mate's, or masked). Ben's free repeats were all within a minute; his charged ones (*bumble bee*, *dragon fly*, *hawkmoth* after *bumblebee*, *dragonfly*, *hawk moth*) were older. A spaced or hyphenated form is the same word. The page says which: "No penalty, as it was moments ago." or the penalty.
- **A word for a prompt that has just changed** is judged against the old prompt for 30 seconds, but only if the player was still looking at the old one. Cal's *ferry* and Ben's *kayak*/*sloop* were sent after their helper had shown the new prompt, so they were judged against it (and didn't fit).

Two things are worth a look all the same:

- **A prompt changing while you type.** On the page, a word goes with the prompt shown when it's sent; if the prompt changes under a player mid-word, a right answer to the old prompt is judged against the new one and can cost 2. With prompts lasting a minute this is easy to hit. The page could send the prompt that was showing when the player started typing.
- **An old repeat costing 2 felt unfair to every agent** ("nothing tells you the word is old"). In a room, where words stay said for weeks, players will hit this far more than in the big world. Options: free for any repeat (a penalty only for words that don't fit), or a longer grace than a minute.

## Lists and dictionary gaps (insects)

- **Missing two-word insects**, all "We don't know" (reported by Dee and others): bark beetle, ground beetle, click beetle, scarab beetle, rhinoceros beetle, carpet beetle, death watch beetle, jewel beetle, burying beetle, leaf beetle, bush cricket, mole cricket, emperor moth, peppered moth, puss moth, carpenter ant, wood ant, mason bee, carpenter bee, robber fly, assassin bug, pill bug, orange tip; and single words *planthopper*, *backswimmer*, *bristletail*, *cutworm*, *firebrat*. Single-word insects were well covered; "X beetle", "X moth", "X bee" phrases are the gap.
- **Spiders on "An insect or bug"**: *house spider* counted (full prompt bonus), *wolf spider* and *garden spider* were accepted as looser answers ("uncounted"), *harvestman* counted; *earth worm* was refused. Owner's decision whether spiders belong; either way *house spider* and *wolf spider* should be treated alike.
- **Other words:** *breadcrumb* isn't in the dictionary (Eve, "Starts and ends with B"); *brimstone* (a butterfly) refused for insects, perhaps deliberately.
- **"Uncounted"** puzzled the agents, but only because they see the API: the page explains it ("It's a looser answer, so it doesn't bring the count down or get the prompt bonus.").

## The world multiplier

It rose from ×1.000 to ×1.003 over about 160 words: right for a room of 52,000 words used up over weeks, and invisible over half an hour. Nothing wrong; worth knowing that in a room's first days only the prompt bonus moves. (The agents' text view didn't show it; the page does.)

## Smaller points

- **"Join a room" took about 3 seconds with no sign anything was happening.** A "Finding you a room…" state on the button.
- **The rooms box says "Play with 60 others in a separate world"**: it's up to 60, and the room was empty. "Play in a room of up to 60" or the room's real count.
- **A miss message outlives its prompt**: Eve's *breadcrumb* reply stayed under the word box after the prompt changed.
- **A long familiar word scores high on length alone** (*tortoiseshell* 41, 13 letters, ×2.3): mostly the rarity placement above.

## Fixes, most important first

1. **Bigger prompts in rooms** (say 40 to 200 answers).
2. **Irregular plurals as families** (knife/knives, mouse/mice, child/children, woodlouse/woodlice and the rest).
3. **British and closed-compound rarity**: a British frequency list, and closed compounds placed by their spaced form (with the rarity review's open item).
4. **The owner's notes**: Just said high on the page (main page and phone width); who's active now on leaderboards; "112 words said" on room cards, plus the percentage below 100%; a Your words list on finished worlds.
5. **Penalties**: judge a word against the prompt showing when the player started typing; reconsider the 2-point cost for an old repeat in rooms.
6. **Insect list gaps**: the two-word insects above; decide on spiders and treat them alike.
7. **Smaller**: a busy state on "Join a room"; the rooms box wording; clear a miss message when the prompt changes; *breadcrumb*.
