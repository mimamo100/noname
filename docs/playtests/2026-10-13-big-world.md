# Playtest: the big world, 13 October 2026

Four Claude agents played the test site's big world (unsaid.gamuli.com) through the API for about 30 minutes each, at a human pace (a word every 10 to 30 seconds), as four kinds of player: Ada (quick and casual), Ben (competitive, rare words), Cal (pokes at edge cases) and Dee (cares about the word lists, reports gaps). The owner played alongside them on a phone as Mimamo. Prompts played: "A bird with no E" (115 answers) and the start of "Letters in alphabetical order, 5+ letters" (81 answers). Full reports from each agent were kept with the session's notes.

Final scores: Ben 879, Dee 719, Ada 592, Cal 288, Mimamo 65, Pinky 51. No HTTP errors or broken responses in about 200 plays.

## The owner's notes

- **The phone layout isn't friendly**: a long list of words comes first and the leaderboard after it, so you scroll a long way to see the scores.
- **"Letters in alphabetical order, 5+ letters" is a really good prompt**: hard to cheat on (short of an LLM), because you have to test each word in your head.
- The agents outscored a human by a wide margin: they find rare words easily, so their sense of what's hard or fair needs a human check.

## 1. Score growth is too steep (all four agreed)

The owner asked about this. What the agents measured:

- **The prompt bonus does all the work.** It's answers at the start ÷ answers unsaid, capped at ×5. On the bird prompt it was ×1.06 at 109 unsaid, ×2 at about 57, ×3 at about 38, and hit the ×5 cap with 23 of 115 still unsaid. It resets to about ×1 on each new prompt.
- **The world multiplier hardly moves:** ×1.0015 to ×1.008 over 30 minutes. It's based on the share of the whole 39,700-word dictionary said, so a daily big world will never get far above ×1.
- **Late words are worth 5 to 20 times early ones.** Early common birds scored 1 to 15 (*owl* 1, *duck* 2, *crow* 3, *sparrow* 10); late easy ones 40 to 84 (*sparrowhawk* 84, *blackcap* 81, *dabchick* 81, *duckling* 46). Ada's first 15 words earned 101 points, her last 14 earned 493. About half the round's points came in the last third of one prompt.
- **The leaderboard is decided by who stays for the end of a prompt**, not by who knows the most words. The leader's gap over Cal went from 64 to 585, nearly all in the second half of the bird prompt. A casual player who plays early and leaves scores almost nothing; arriving mid-prompt is the best time to join.
- **The flat 2-point miss penalty makes guessing almost free late on**, when words are worth 40 to 80.

What they suggest: lower the prompt bonus cap to about ×2.5 to ×3, or make it grow more gently (for example the square root of start ÷ unsaid), so rarity and knowledge do more of the work. All four liked that the end of a prompt gets tense and worth fighting for; the problem is the size of the swing. DESIGN.md already names this ("One late, rare word shouldn't outscore a whole day of early play... the caps are the first lever").

## 2. Messages and penalties

- **"Doesn't fit the prompt" never says why** (all four). Ada took 11 penalties for birds containing an E before realising. Suggest naming the broken rule: "has an E", "too short (5+ letters)".
- **"Already said" costs 2 points even when someone said it seconds earlier.** Cal paid 34 points for 17 of these; it punishes being slow, not being wrong, and will bite in busy worlds. Suggest no penalty, or none within a few seconds.
- **The "already said" check runs before the letter rule**, so *flamingoes* (has an E) was reported as already said.
- **A rejected phrase comes back squashed**: "barn owl" is rejected as *barnowl*, so players think the game mangled their word. Echo what they typed; and for a word that's not in the dictionary, a kinder "We don't know that one. Report it?" (Dee).
- **Scores drop on the leaderboard with no explanation** (miss penalties), which looks like a bug to other players.

## 3. Word lists

- **Two-word British birds are missing**, the first thing a UK player hits: *barn owl*, *tawny owl*, *song thrush*, *blue tit*, *great tit*, *coal tit*, *marsh tit*, *snowy owl*, *pied wagtail*. Phrases now count and are typed naturally, but the bird list was never given its phrases. Note *blue tit* etc. also interact with *tit* being masked.
- **Missing birds:** *myna* (while *mynah* and *mina* are in), *gadwall*, *scaup*, *hoatzin*, *junco*, *bulbul*, *trogon*, *potoo*, *kingbird*, *cowbird*, *bobolink*, *jacana*, *caracara*; *tui* is in the dictionary but not the bird list; *mandarin* (the duck) is borderline.
- **The category name counts as an answer**: *bird* was accepted for "A bird", *vegetables* is an answer for "A vegetable". *Poultry*, *raptor* and *songbird* are listed as birds; *iceberg* as a vegetable (lettuce) is arguable.
- **Word families cross meanings**: *bunting* used up *bunt*, *knot* used up *knotted*, *rail* used up *railing*, *hobby* used up *hobbies*, *crow* used up *crowing*. One bird can burn an unrelated word for later prompts.
- **Missing plurals**: *starlings*, *buntings*, *ibises* aren't in the dictionary (the plurals pass only covered words ENABLE knows, and the bird plurals of new words weren't all added). *Knotty* is missing while *knotted* is in.

## 4. Prompts and pacing

- **Prompts run long**: 25 minutes for the birds, an hour on the clock for the alphabetical one. The bird prompt sat for its last 6 to 7 minutes with only obscure answers left (*guan*, *ani*, *brant*, *squab*), which is dead time, especially for a lunchtime phone player. Consider ending a prompt early when it stalls, or shorter prompts.
- "A bird with no E" was liked by everyone: lots of answers, and the E catches you out (*heron*, *emu*, *pelican*).

## What worked

Every request succeeded. Capitals, typos (free), repeating your own word (free), plurals of your own and others' words, phrases with a space or hyphen when the joined word exists (*night jar*), and compounds containing a used word (*skylark* after *lark*) all behaved as designed. Masked words (*tit*, *cock*, *shag*) were accepted. Counts went down by one per answer.

## Suggested fixes, most valuable first

1. Lower the prompt bonus cap (or flatten its curve), and reconsider the flat miss penalty late in a prompt.
2. Say why a word doesn't fit, and don't charge for "already said" by someone else.
3. Add the two-word birds and the missing birds; take *bird*, *vegetables*, *poultry* out of their own categories (and check the other categories for their own names).
4. Echo what the player typed in rejections.
5. End stalled prompts sooner.
6. Phone layout: the leaderboard (or the player's own position) near the top.
7. Stop word families crossing meanings where it's easy (a list of exceptions, like *bunting*, *knot*, *rail*, *hobby*).
